# Overview

Androgen receptors (AR) are nuclear receptors that are activated by androgens such as testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT). AR plays a crucial role in the development and maintenance of male characteristics and is also involved in various physiological processes in both males and females. AR is an important target for endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) that can interfere with androgen signaling.

# Receptor Characteristics

## Gene and Protein

- **Gene**: AR (androgen receptor)
- **Protein**: Nuclear receptor subfamily 3, group C, member 4 (NR3C4)
- **Location**: Chromosome X (humans)
- **Structure**: Nuclear receptor with DNA-binding and ligand-binding domains
- **Ligands**: Testosterone, dihydrotestosterone (DHT), endocrine disruptors

## Signaling Pathways

AR signaling involves multiple mechanisms:

### Classical Genomic Pathway

1. **Ligand Binding**: Androgen or endocrine disruptor binds to AR
2. **Dimerization**: AR monomers form homodimers
3. **Translocation**: AR dimer translocates to nucleus
4. **DNA Binding**: AR dimer binds to androgen response elements (AREs) in target gene promoters
5. **Transcription Regulation**: Recruits coactivators or corepressors to regulate gene expression

### Non-Genomic Pathway

- **Membrane-associated AR**: Rapid signaling through second messengers
- **Signal Transduction**: Activation of MAPK, PI3K-Akt pathways
- **Cellular Responses**: Cell proliferation, survival, and migration

# Mechanisms of Endocrine Disruption

## BPA and BPA Analogs

BPA and its analogs can interfere with androgen receptor signaling through:

- **Competitive Binding**: Displace androgens from AR binding sites
- **Antagonistic Activity**: Block androgen-induced transcriptional activation
- **Partial Agonism**: Act as weak agonists in some cellular contexts
- **Receptor Degradation**: Enhance AR protein degradation
- **Cross-Talk**: Interfere with other steroid receptor pathways

## Specific Analog Effects

- **BPCcl**: Exhibits strong antiandrogenic activity
- **BPAF**: Shows mixed agonist/antagonist activity
- **BPZ**: May have androgenic activity in some contexts
- **Species Differences**: Vary in their AR interaction profiles

# Toxicological Implications

## Health Effects

AR disruption contributes to:

- **Reproductive System**: Reduced fertility, developmental disorders
- **Musculoskeletal System**: Muscle wasting, bone health effects
- **Cardiovascular System**: Vascular function changes
- **Neurological Effects**: Behavioral changes, cognitive function
- **Cancer**: Prostate cancer risk modulation

## Developmental Effects

- **Fetal Development**: Disruption of sexual differentiation
- **Puberty**: Delayed or altered pubertal development
- **Adult Health**: Long-term effects on reproductive function

# Species-Specific Effects

- **Receptor Sequence**: AR sequence varies between species
- **Ligand Specificity**: Different binding affinities for BPA analogs
- **Toxicological Outcomes**: Species-specific health effects
- **Regulatory Implications**: Differences in sensitivity to endocrine disruptors
