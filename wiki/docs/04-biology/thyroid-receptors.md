# Overview

Thyroid hormone receptors (TRs) are nuclear receptors that mediate the effects of thyroid hormones (T3 and T4). There are two main types of thyroid hormone receptors: TRα (thyroid hormone receptor alpha) and TRβ (thyroid hormone receptor beta). These receptors play crucial roles in regulating metabolism, growth, development, and neurological function. TRs are important targets for endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) that can interfere with thyroid hormone signaling.

# Receptor Types

## Thyroid Hormone Receptor Alpha (TRα)

- **Gene**: THRA
- **Isoforms**: TRα1, TRα2, TRα3 (dominant negative)
- **Location**: Chromosome 17 (humans)
- **Function**: Regulates metabolism, heart function, and development
- **Expression**: High in heart, brain, and skeletal muscle
- **Toxicological Relevance**: Target for endocrine disruptors affecting metabolism

## Thyroid Hormone Receptor Beta (TRβ)

- **Gene**: THRB
- **Isoforms**: TRβ1, TRβ2
- **Location**: Chromosome 3 (humans)
- **Function**: Regulates metabolism, lipid homeostasis, and neurological function
- **Expression**: High in liver, kidney, and brain
- **Toxicological Relevance**: Important for endocrine disruption effects on metabolism

# Mechanisms of Action

## Classical Genomic Pathway

1. **Ligand Binding**: Thyroid hormone or endocrine disruptor binds to TR
2. **Dimerization**: TR monomers form homodimers (TRα-TRα, TRβ-TRβ) or heterodimers (TRα-TRβ, TR-RXR)
3. **DNA Binding**: TR dimer binds to thyroid hormone response elements (TREs) in target gene promoters
4. **Transcription Regulation**: Recruits coactivators or corepressors to regulate gene expression

## Non-Genomic Pathway

- **Membrane-associated TRs**: Rapid signaling through second messengers
- **Signal Transduction**: Activation of MAPK, PI3K-Akt pathways
- **Cellular Responses**: Cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic regulation

# Mechanisms of Endocrine Disruption

## BPA and BPA Analogs

BPA and its analogs can interfere with thyroid hormone receptor signaling through:

- **Competitive Binding**: Displace thyroid hormones from TR binding sites
- **Altered Transcription**: Modify gene expression patterns through non-physiological receptor activation
- **Receptor Cross-Talk**: Interfere with other nuclear receptor pathways
- **Thyroid Hormone Synthesis**: Alter genes involved in thyroid hormone production and metabolism

## Specific Effects

- **Gene Expression**: Changes in thyroid hormone-responsive genes
- **Metabolic Effects**: Disruption of energy metabolism and lipid homeostasis
- **Developmental Effects**: Alterations in neurological development
- **Hormone Levels**: Changes in circulating thyroid hormone concentrations

# Toxicological Implications

## Health Effects

TR disruption contributes to:

- **Metabolic System**: Obesity, diabetes, metabolic syndrome
- **Neurological System**: Developmental disorders, cognitive function
- **Cardiovascular System**: Heart rate regulation, blood pressure
- **Developmental Effects**: Fetal brain development, growth patterns
- **Endocrine System**: Thyroid hormone homeostasis disruption

## Developmental Effects

- **Fetal Development**: Disruption of neurological development
- **Child Development**: Cognitive and motor function effects
- **Adult Health**: Long-term metabolic and neurological effects

# Species-Specific Effects

- **Receptor Sequence**: TR sequence varies between species
- **Ligand Specificity**: Different binding affinities for BPA analogs
- **Toxicological Outcomes**: Species-specific health effects
- **Regulatory Implications**: Differences in sensitivity to endocrine disruptors
