# Overview

Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are a group of nuclear receptors that function as transcription factors regulating the expression of genes involved in lipid metabolism, glucose homeostasis, and inflammation. There are three main PPAR isoforms: PPARα, PPARγ, and PPARβ/δ (also called PPARδ). PPARs play crucial roles in energy homeostasis and are important targets for endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) such as BPA and its analogs.

# Receptor Isoforms

## PPAR Alpha (PPARα)

- **Gene**: PPARGCA
- **Location**: Chromosome 22 (humans)
- **Function**: Regulates fatty acid oxidation, lipid metabolism
- **Expression**: High in liver, kidney, heart, and muscle
- **Toxicological Relevance**: Target for chemicals affecting metabolism

## PPAR Gamma (PPARγ)

- **Gene**: PPARG
- **Location**: Chromosome 3 (humans)
- **Function**: Regulates adipogenesis, glucose metabolism, inflammation
- **Expression**: High in adipose tissue, macrophages, colon
- **Toxicological Relevance**: Important for metabolic and inflammatory effects

## PPAR Delta/Beta (PPARβ/δ)

- **Gene**: PPARD
- **Location**: Chromosome 6 (humans)
- **Function**: Regulates fatty acid oxidation, energy homeostasis
- **Expression**: Ubiquitous, high in skeletal muscle, brain
- **Toxicological Relevance**: Target for chemicals affecting energy metabolism

# Mechanisms of Action

## Classical Genomic Pathway

1. **Ligand Binding**: Fatty acid or endocrine disruptor binds to PPAR
2. **Heterodimerization**: PPAR forms heterodimer with RXR (retinoid X receptor)
3. **DNA Binding**: PPAR-RXR heterodimer binds to PPAR response elements (PPREs) in target gene promoters
4. **Transcription Regulation**: Recruits coactivators or corepressors to regulate gene expression

## Cross-Talk with Other Pathways

- **LXR**: Liver X receptor cross-talk
- **LHR-1**: Liver receptor homolog-1 interactions
- **Nrf2**: Antioxidant response element regulation
- **Inflammatory Pathways**: NF-κB and AP-1 modulation

# Mechanisms of Endocrine Disruption

## BPA and BPA Analogs

BPA and its analogs can interfere with PPAR signaling through:

- **Direct Activation**: Binding to PPARs and triggering transcriptional activation
- **Cross-Talk**: Interfering with other nuclear receptor pathways
- **Metabolic Effects**: Altering lipid and glucose metabolism
- **Inflammatory Effects**: Modulating inflammatory response pathways

## Specific Isoform Effects

- **PPARα**: Effects on fatty acid oxidation and lipid metabolism
- **PPARγ**: Effects on adipogenesis and glucose metabolism
- **PPARβ/δ**: Effects on energy homeostasis and inflammation
- **Species Differences**: Vary in their PPAR interaction profiles

# Toxicological Implications

## Health Effects

PPAR disruption contributes to:

- **Metabolic System**: Obesity, diabetes, metabolic syndrome
- **Cardiovascular System**: Lipid profile changes, atherosclerosis
- **Inflammatory System**: Chronic inflammation, autoimmune diseases
- **Developmental Effects**: Fetal programming of metabolic disorders
- **Cancer**: Tumor progression and metabolism

## Metabolic Effects

- **Lipid Metabolism**: Changes in cholesterol and triglyceride levels
- **Glucose Metabolism**: Insulin resistance and diabetes risk
- **Energy Homeostasis**: Effects on weight regulation and energy expenditure
- **Adipogenesis**: Alterations in fat cell development and function

# Species-Specific Effects

- **Receptor Sequence**: PPAR sequence varies between species
- **Ligand Specificity**: Different binding affinities for BPA analogs
- **Toxicological Outcomes**: Species-specific health effects
- **Regulatory Implications**: Differences in sensitivity to endocrine disruptors
