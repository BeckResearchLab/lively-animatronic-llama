# Overview

Estrogen receptors (ERs) are a group of nuclear receptors that are activated by the hormone estrogen. There are two main types: estrogen receptor alpha (ERα) and estrogen receptor beta (ERβ). These receptors play crucial roles in the regulation of growth, differentiation, and function of many tissues, including reproductive tissues, bone, cardiovascular system, and central nervous system. ERs are also important targets for endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) such as bisphenol A (BPA) and its analogs.

# Evidence and Details

## Receptor Types and Distribution

Estrogen receptors are ligand-activated transcription factors that regulate gene expression:

### ERα (Estrogen Receptor Alpha)
- **Gene:** ESR1
- **Tissue distribution:** Ubiquitous but particularly high in uterus, liver, kidney, and hypothalamus
- **Function:** Associated with reproductive function, bone maintenance, and cardiovascular protection
- **Binding affinity:** Generally higher affinity for most estrogenic compounds compared to ERβ

### ERβ (Estrogen Receptor Beta)
- **Gene:** ESR2
- **Tissue distribution:** High in prostate, ovary, brain, lung, and gastrointestinal tract
- **Function:** Associated with neuroprotection, anti-inflammatory effects, and regulation of energy balance
- **Binding affinity:** Often shows different selectivity profile than ERα for various ligands

## Mechanisms of Action

Estrogen receptors function through several mechanisms:

1. **Classical genomic pathway**: Ligand binding leads to receptor dimerization, interaction with co-regulators, and transcriptional regulation
2. **Non-genomic pathway**: Rapid signaling events mediated by membrane-associated receptors
3. **Epigenetic regulation**: Ligand binding can influence DNA methylation and histone modification patterns
4. **Cross-talk with other signaling pathways**: Interaction with MAPK, PI3K-Akt, and other pathways

## Endocrine Disruption

Estrogen receptors are major targets for endocrine-disrupting chemicals:

- **BPA**: Weak agonist for both ERα and ERβ, with preferential binding to ERβ
- **BPAF**: Stronger agonist than BPA, particularly for ERα
- **Other analogs**: Varying affinity profiles depending on chemical structure
- **Mechanisms**: Competitive binding, altered receptor expression, disrupted co-regulator interactions

## Epigenetic Effects

Emerging evidence suggests that BPA and its analogs can induce epigenetic changes at estrogen receptor loci:

- **DNA methylation**: Changes in methylation patterns at ERα and ERβ promoter regions
- **Histone modifications**: Alterations in histone acetylation and methylation states
- **Long-term effects**: Potential for transgenerational effects through epigenetic programming
- **Gene expression**: Resulting changes in estrogen receptor expression and downstream target genes
