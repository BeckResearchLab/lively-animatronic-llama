# Overview

The pregnane X receptor (PXR), also known as NR1I2, is a nuclear receptor that regulates the expression of genes involved in drug metabolism and transport. PXR is activated by a wide variety of endogenous and exogenous ligands, including steroids, drugs, and environmental chemicals. PXR plays a crucial role in the body's response to xenobiotics and is an important target for endocrine-disrupting chemicals (EDCs) such as BPA and its analogs.

# Receptor Characteristics

## Gene and Protein

- **Gene**: NR1I2 (nuclear receptor subfamily 1, group I, member 2)
- **Protein**: Pregnane X receptor
- **Location**: Chromosome 3 (humans)
- **Structure**: Nuclear receptor with DNA-binding and ligand-binding domains
- **Ligands**: Steroids, drugs, environmental chemicals, BPA analogs

## Signaling Pathways

PXR signaling involves multiple mechanisms:

### Classical Genomic Pathway

1. **Ligand Binding**: Steroid, drug, or endocrine disruptor binds to PXR
2. **Heterodimerization**: PXR forms heterodimer with RXR (retinoid X receptor)
3. **DNA Binding**: PXR-RXR heterodimer binds to PXR response elements (PXREs) in target gene promoters
4. **Transcription Regulation**: Recruits coactivators to regulate gene expression

### Target Genes

- **Cytochrome P450 enzymes**: CYP3A4, CYP2B6, CYP2C9
- **Drug transporters**: MDR1, MRP2, OATP
- **Phase II enzymes**: UGT1A1, GST
- **Nuclear receptors**: CAR, FXR

# Mechanisms of Endocrine Disruption

## BPA and BPA Analogs

BPA and its analogs can interfere with PXR signaling through:

- **Direct Activation**: Binding to PXR and triggering transcriptional activation
- **Gene Expression**: Induction of drug metabolism and transport genes
- **Xenobiotic Response**: Modulation of the body's response to environmental chemicals
- **Species Differences**: Vary significantly in their PXR interaction profiles

## Species-Specific Effects

PXR shows remarkable species-specific differences:

- **Ligand Specificity**: Different binding affinities for various chemicals
- **Gene Regulation**: Species-specific patterns of target gene activation
- **Toxicological Outcomes**: Vary significantly between species
- **Regulatory Implications**: Differences in sensitivity to endocrine disruptors

### Human PXR

- **Broad Ligand Specificity**: Activates by many drugs and environmental chemicals
- **Limited Steroid Response**: Less responsive to endogenous steroids
- **Toxicological Profile**: Human-specific effects of PXR activation

### Rodent PXR

- **Narrow Ligand Specificity**: More selective ligand binding
- **Steroid Response**: Responsive to pregnenolone and other steroids
- **Toxicological Profile**: Different effects compared to human PXR

# Toxicological Implications

## Health Effects

PXR disruption contributes to:

- **Drug Metabolism**: Changes in drug clearance and efficacy
- **Xenobiotic Response**: Altered response to environmental chemicals
- **Endocrine System**: Interference with steroid hormone metabolism
- **Cancer**: Effects on drug-resistant tumor cells
- **Toxicity**: Modulation of chemical toxicity and carcinogenicity

## Mechanistic Effects

- **Drug-Drug Interactions**: Changes in cytochrome P450 enzyme activity
- **Chemical Toxicity**: Modulation of chemical metabolism and clearance
- **Hormone Metabolism**: Effects on steroid hormone homeostasis
- **Transport**: Alterations in drug and chemical transport
