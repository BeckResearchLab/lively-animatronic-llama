# Evidence Extraction Workflow

## Overview

The Evidence Extraction Workflow is a structured process designed to systematically identify, extract, and synthesize evidence from various sources to support computational toxicology assessments. This workflow ensures that the extracted evidence is accurate, relevant, and traceable, facilitating robust decision-making in toxicological evaluations.

## Scope and Notes

This workflow is applicable to the extraction of evidence from scientific literature, databases, and other relevant sources. It is designed to be adaptable to different types of evidence, including experimental data, computational models, and expert opinions. The workflow emphasizes the importance of transparency, reproducibility, and the integration of multiple data sources to ensure comprehensive evidence synthesis.

## Key Steps in the Evidence Extraction Workflow

### 1. Definition of Evidence Scope

**Purpose**: Clearly define the scope of evidence required for the assessment.

**Actions**:
- Identify the specific research question or toxicological endpoint of interest.
- Define the types of evidence to be extracted (e.g., experimental data, computational models, literature reviews).
- Establish inclusion and exclusion criteria for the evidence.

**Output**: A well-defined evidence scope document outlining the research question, types of evidence, and criteria for inclusion and exclusion.

### 2. Source Identification

**Purpose**: Identify and select relevant sources of evidence.

**Actions**:
- Conduct a comprehensive search of scientific literature, databases, and other relevant sources.
- Use structured search strategies to ensure completeness and relevance.
- Apply inclusion and exclusion criteria to filter sources.

**Output**: A list of relevant sources that meet the predefined criteria.

### 3. Data Extraction

**Purpose**: Extract relevant data from the identified sources.

**Actions**:
- Develop standardized data extraction forms or templates.
- Extract key information such as study design, methods, results, and conclusions.
- Ensure consistency and accuracy in data extraction.

**Output**: Extracted data in a structured format, ready for analysis.

### 4. Data Quality Assessment

**Purpose**: Assess the quality and reliability of the extracted data.

**Actions**:
- Evaluate the quality of each source using predefined criteria (e.g., study design, sample size, methodological rigor).
- Identify potential biases or limitations in the data.
- Assign a quality score or rating to each source.

**Output**: A quality assessment report for each source, including scores and notes on potential biases.

### 5. Data Synthesis

**Purpose**: Synthesize the extracted data to generate insights and support decision-making.

**Actions**:
- Combine data from multiple sources to identify patterns, trends, or inconsistencies.
- Use statistical or computational methods to analyze the data.
- Generate summaries or visualizations to facilitate interpretation.

**Output**: Synthesized data and insights, presented in a clear and concise format.

### 6. Documentation and Reporting

**Purpose**: Document the entire evidence extraction process and report the findings.

**Actions**:
- Maintain detailed records of all steps, including search strategies, data extraction, and quality assessments.
- Prepare a comprehensive report summarizing the findings and the evidence extraction process.
- Ensure transparency by providing access to raw data and methodologies.

**Output**: A final report documenting the evidence extraction process, findings, and supporting data.

## Tools and Technologies

The Evidence Extraction Workflow can be supported by various tools and technologies, including:

- **Literature Search Tools**: PubMed, Europe PMC, and other scientific databases.
- **Data Extraction Tools**: Custom scripts, natural language processing (NLP) tools, and data extraction software.
- **Quality Assessment Tools**: Checklists and guidelines for evaluating the quality of scientific studies.
- **Data Synthesis Tools**: Statistical software, visualization tools, and computational models.
