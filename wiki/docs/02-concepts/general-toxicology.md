## Overview

Toxicology is the scientific study of the adverse effects of chemicals on living organisms. It encompasses the study of the harmful effects of chemicals, their mechanisms of action, and the development of methods to assess and mitigate chemical risks.

## Fundamental Principles

### Dose-Response Relationship

A fundamental principle of toxicology is the dose-response relationship:

- **No observed adverse effect level (NOAEL)**: The highest dose at which no adverse effects are observed
- **Lowest observed adverse effect level (LOAEL)**: The lowest dose at which adverse effects are observed
- **Benchmark dose (BMD)**: Statistical estimate of the dose corresponding to a specific level of adverse effect. See [Benchmark Dose Modeling](../../08-models-and-methods/benchmark-dose-modeling.md) for details.
- **Safety factor**: Factor applied to NOAEL or BMD to account for uncertainties in risk assessment

### Paracelsus' Principle

"All substances are poisons; there is none which is not a poison. The right dose differentiates a poison and a remedy." This principle emphasizes that the dose determines whether a substance is harmful or safe.

## Toxicological Endpoints

Toxicological endpoints are specific measures of adverse effects used in toxicity testing:

1. **Acute toxicity**: Effects following short-term exposure
2. **Chronic toxicity**: Effects following long-term exposure
3. **Developmental toxicity**: Effects on developing organisms
4. **Reproductive toxicity**: Effects on reproductive function
5. **Genotoxicity**: Effects on genetic material
6. **Carcinogenicity**: Potential to cause cancer
7. **Neurotoxicity**: Effects on the nervous system
8. **Immunotoxicity**: Effects on the immune system

## Mechanisms of Toxicity

Toxic effects can result from various mechanisms:

- **Direct chemical interaction**: Binding to biological targets (receptors, enzymes, DNA)
- **Metabolic activation**: Conversion of chemicals to more toxic metabolites
- **Oxidative stress**: Generation of reactive oxygen species
- **Disruption of biological pathways**: Interference with normal cellular processes
- **Immune system modulation**: Alteration of immune responses

## Risk Assessment Framework

The traditional risk assessment framework consists of four main steps:

1. **Hazard identification**: Identification of adverse health effects
2. **Dose-response assessment**: Characterization of the relationship between dose and response
3. **Exposure assessment**: Characterization of exposure patterns
4. **Risk characterization**: Integration of hazard, dose-response, and exposure information

## New Approach Methodologies

New approach methodologies (NAMs) represent modern approaches to toxicology that complement or replace traditional methods:

- **In vitro methods**: Cell-based and tissue-based assays
- **In silico methods**: Computational modeling and prediction
- **Omics technologies**: Genomics, proteomics, metabolomics
- **High-throughput screening**: Rapid assessment of chemical effects
- **Adverse outcome pathways**: Mechanistic frameworks linking molecular events to adverse outcomes

## Challenges in Toxicology

Key challenges in the field include:

- **Complex exposures**: Real-world exposures often involve mixtures of chemicals
- **Individual variability**: Differences in susceptibility among individuals
- **Data gaps**: Limited toxicological data for many chemicals
- **Emerging technologies**: Rapid advances in technology require continuous adaptation
- **Regulatory acceptance**: Integration of new methods into regulatory frameworks
