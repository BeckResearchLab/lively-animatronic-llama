## Overview

Regulatory initiatives play a crucial role in advancing computational toxicology by promoting the development, validation, and acceptance of new approach methodologies (NAMs) and alternative methods to traditional animal testing. These initiatives support the transition toward next-generation risk assessment (NGRA) and the integration of computational approaches into regulatory decision-making.

## Scope and Notes

This page covers:
- Key regulatory agencies and their initiatives
- Major programs and guidance documents
- International collaboration and harmonization efforts
- Challenges in regulatory acceptance of computational methods
- Future directions for regulatory support

Regulatory initiatives should not be confused with individual regulatory decisions. The focus is on broader programs and policies that shape the regulatory landscape for computational toxicology.

## Major Regulatory Agencies and Initiatives

### U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA)

**Key Initiatives:**
- **ToxCast Program**: High-throughput screening of environmental chemicals
- **CompTox Chemistry Dashboard**: Public database for chemical toxicity data
- **New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) Roadmap**: Strategy for integrating NAMs into regulatory decision-making
- **Endocrine Disruptor Screening Program**: Use of computational and in vitro methods
- **Toxic Substances Control Act (TSCA)**: Amendments call for the development of NAMs to reduce dependence on animal testing

**Guidance Documents:**
- EPA's guidance on the use of in vitro data in risk assessment
- Framework for evaluating NAMs for regulatory purposes
- Integration of computational toxicology into chemical evaluations

### European Union (EU) Agencies

**Key Initiatives:**
- **REACH Regulation**: Encourages alternative methods to animal testing
- **EU-ToxRisk Project**: Integrated testing strategies for toxicity assessment
- **SEURAT-1 Cluster**: Research on alternative methods for regulatory testing
- **ECHA Guidance**: On the use of in vitro and in silico methods

**Guidance Documents:**
- ECHA guidance on integrated testing strategies
- EU reference laboratory network for alternative methods
- Validation criteria for NAMs in regulatory contexts

### Other International Agencies

**Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD):**
- **QSAR Toolbox**: Software for predicting chemical properties
- **Adverse Outcome Pathway (AOP) Program**: Development of AOPs for regulatory use
- **Guidance on Integrated Approaches to Testing and Assessment (IATA)**

**International Cooperation on Alternative Test Methods (ICATM):**
- Global collaboration on alternative methods development
- Harmonization of validation approaches
- Capacity building for regulatory acceptance

## Key Programs and Initiatives

### High-Throughput Screening Programs

- **ToxCast (EPA)**: Large-scale screening of environmental chemicals
- **Tox21 (NIH/EPA)**: Collaboration on toxicity testing
- **EU-ToxRisk**: Integrated testing strategies for toxicity assessment

### Computational Toxicology Programs

- **CompTox Chemistry Dashboard (EPA)**: Public database for chemical toxicity data
- **OECD QSAR Toolbox**: Software for predicting chemical properties
- **US EPA's NAMs Roadmap**: Strategy for integrating NAMs into regulatory decision-making

### Adverse Outcome Pathway Programs

- **OECD AOP Program**: Development of AOPs for regulatory use
- **AOP-Wiki**: Collaborative platform for AOP development
- **AOP Knowledge Base**: Central repository for AOP information

## Challenges in Regulatory Acceptance

### Validation Requirements

- Need for clear criteria for NAMs validation
- Challenges in establishing confidence in computational predictions
- Issues with reproducibility and standardization

### Data Integration

- Need for standardized data formats and reporting
- Challenges in integrating data from diverse sources
- Issues with data harmonization and interoperability

### Regulatory Frameworks

- Need for updated regulatory guidelines and frameworks
- Challenges in incorporating NAMs into existing risk assessment paradigms
- Issues with legal and policy barriers

### International Harmonization

- Need for global consensus on validation approaches
- Challenges in addressing jurisdictional differences
- Issues with capacity building in developing countries

## Future Directions

- Development of clear validation criteria for NAMs
- Integration of computational approaches into regulatory decision-making
- Enhanced international collaboration and harmonization
- Capacity building for regulatory acceptance of NAMs
- Development of predictive models for emerging technologies
- Application of NAMs to complex mixtures and environmental exposures
