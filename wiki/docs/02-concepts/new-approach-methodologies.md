# New Approach Methodologies (NAMs)

New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) represent modern approaches to toxicology that complement or replace traditional animal-based methods. These methodologies leverage in silico, in vitro, and targeted in vivo approaches to assess chemical safety and toxicity.

## Scope and Notes

This page covers:
- Definition and scope of NAMs
- Types of NAMs and their applications
- Integration of NAMs in chemical safety assessment
- Regulatory acceptance and frameworks supporting NAMs
- Emerging trends and future directions

## Types of NAMs

### In Silico Methods

Computational approaches that predict toxicity based on chemical structure and biological data:
- Quantitative Structure-Activity Relationship (QSAR) models
- Physiologically-Based Pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models
- Machine learning and artificial intelligence approaches
- Molecular docking and simulation

### In Vitro Methods

Laboratory-based approaches using isolated cells, tissues, or cell components:
- High-throughput screening assays
- Organ-on-chip technologies
- 3D cell cultures
- Stem cell-based models

### Targeted In Vivo Methods

Animal studies designed to provide specific, relevant information with reduced animal use:
- Refined animal testing protocols
- Biomarker validation studies
- Translational research models

## Regulatory Frameworks

### REACH Regulation

The Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) regulation encourages the use of alternative methods to animal testing and provides a framework for integrating NAMs into chemical safety assessment.

## Benefits of NAMs

- **Scientific Rigor**: Provides mechanistic insights and human-relevant data
- **Ethical**: Reduces or eliminates animal suffering
- **Practical**: Faster, more cost-effective, and scalable
- **Regulatory**: Supports next-generation risk assessment frameworks
- **Transparency**: Enables clear documentation of decision-making processes

## Challenges and Future Directions

- **Data Integration**: Combining data from diverse sources and methodologies
- **Validation**: Establishing confidence in new methods for regulatory use
- **Acceptance**: Overcoming skepticism and resistance to change
- **Standardization**: Developing common protocols and reporting formats
- **Regulatory Harmonization**: Ensuring consistent acceptance across jurisdictions
