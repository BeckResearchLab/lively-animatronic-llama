# Overview

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) 2025 guidance provides a comprehensive framework for applying read-across methods in food and feed safety assessments. This guidance represents a significant step toward formalizing read-across as an accepted approach in regulatory toxicology for food safety applications.

## Key Features

> **Claim**: EFSA's 2025 guidance provides a seven-step workflow for food and feed safety, emphasizing transparency, NAM integration, and uncertainty analysis.
> **Citation**: [cit-001](#citation-001)

### Seven-Step Workflow

The EFSA guidance outlines a structured seven-step process:

1. **Problem Formulation**: Define the assessment purpose, target chemical, endpoint of interest, and intended use
2. **Data Collection**: Gather existing data for both target and potential source chemicals
3. **Source Substance Identification**: Identify structurally and mechanistically similar chemicals
4. **Similarity Assessment**: Evaluate chemical, toxicokinetic, and toxicodynamic similarities
5. **Read-Across Justification**: Develop scientific rationale for the read-across approach
6. **Data Gap Filling**: Apply read-across to fill identified data gaps
7. **Uncertainty Assessment**: Characterize and quantify uncertainties in the read-across process

## Scientific Rigor

### Mechanistic Similarity Requirements

> **Claim**: EFSA emphasizes the need for mechanistic plausibility in read-across justifications.
> **Citation**: [cit-002](#citation-002)

The guidance requires:
- Clear mechanistic understanding of the endpoint
- Evidence of shared biological pathways
- Consideration of molecular initiating events
- Integration with adverse outcome pathways (AOPs)

### NAM Integration

> **Claim**: EFSA strongly encourages the use of new approach methods (NAMs) to strengthen read-across arguments.
> **Citation**: [cit-003](#citation-003)

Accepted NAMs include:
- In vitro toxicity assays
- Computational models and QSAR approaches
- Physiologically based pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models
- Omics technologies (transcriptomics, proteomics)
- Adverse outcome pathway (AOP) frameworks

### Uncertainty Analysis

> **Claim**: EFSA provides dedicated methods for uncertainty assessment in read-across.
> **Citation**: [cit-004](#citation-004)

Uncertainty assessment components:
- Structural similarity uncertainty
- Data quality and reliability assessment
- Extrapolation uncertainty quantification
- Weight-of-evidence evaluation
- Confidence level classification

## Practical Implementation

### Structured Templates

> **Claim**: EFSA offers structured templates and tools to support read-across implementation.
> **Citation**: [cit-005](#citation-005)

Available tools include:
- Data matrix templates for similarity assessment
- Uncertainty assessment worksheets
- Reporting format guidelines
- Case study examples and templates

### Case Studies

> **Claim**: EFSA provides examples and case studies illustrating successful read-across applications.
> **Citation**: [cit-006](#citation-006)

Case study domains include:
- Food additive safety assessments
- Pesticide residue evaluations
- Natural toxin risk assessments
- Processing contaminant evaluations

## Regulatory Impact

> **Claim**: EFSA's guidance signals a shift toward formal acceptance of read-across in food/feed risk assessments.
> **Citation**: [cit-007](#citation-007)

Key regulatory implications:
- Formal recognition of read-across in EFSA evaluations
- Clear criteria for regulatory acceptance
- Transparency requirements for submissions
- Integration with existing EFSA risk assessment frameworks

## Comparison with Other Frameworks

### EFSA vs. ECHA RAAF

- **Scope**: EFSA focuses on food/feed safety; RAAF targets industrial chemicals under REACH
- **Structure**: EFSA uses a linear workflow; RAAF employs scenario-based assessment elements
- **NAM Integration**: EFSA explicitly encourages NAMs; RAAF is compatible but less prescriptive
- **Uncertainty**: EFSA has dedicated uncertainty analysis; RAAF integrates uncertainty into assessment elements

### EFSA vs. GRAP Principles

- **Approach**: EFSA provides specific guidance; GRAP offers general best-practice principles
- **Transparency**: Both emphasize transparency but with different reporting formats
- **Mechanistic Focus**: Both require mechanistic justification but with different levels of detail
- **Regulatory Context**: EFSA is EFSA-specific; GRAP aims for broader applicability
