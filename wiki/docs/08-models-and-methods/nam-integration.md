# NAM Integration with Read-Across

## Overview

New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) play a crucial role in enhancing the reliability and regulatory acceptance of read-across (RAx) approaches. NAMs provide additional data and mechanistic understanding that can support and validate RAx predictions, particularly when traditional data sources are limited.

## Key Concepts

### Definition

NAM integration involves the strategic use of in vitro assays, in silico models, and other non-animal methods to generate data that supports read-across justifications. This integration helps address data gaps and provides mechanistic evidence for similarity assessments.

> **Claim**: NAMs can support RAx by providing data to confirm biological mechanism similarities
> **Citation**: [cit-007](#citation-007)

### Types of NAMs

1. **In Vitro Assays**: Biological assays that measure specific endpoints or mechanisms
2. **In Silico Models**: Computational models that predict toxicity based on chemical structure or biological data
3. **Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic (PBPK) Models**: Models that simulate the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of chemicals
4. **Adverse Outcome Pathway (AOP) Frameworks**: Mechanistic frameworks that link molecular initiating events to adverse outcomes

> **Claim**: NAMs should be performed specifically to demonstrate RAx hypotheses
> **Citation**: [cit-008](#citation-008)

> **Claim**: NAMs include in vitro assays, in silico models, and computational approaches
> **Citation**: [cit-009](#citation-009)

## Methodologies

### Data Generation

NAMs are used to generate data that can support RAx by:

1. **Confirming Mechanistic Similarity**: Demonstrating that source and target chemicals share similar biological mechanisms
2. **Filling Data Gaps**: Providing data for endpoints where experimental data is lacking
3. **Validating Predictions**: Confirming that RAx predictions are consistent with experimental observations

### Integration Strategies

1. **Targeted Data Generation**: Performing NAMs specifically to address identified data gaps in RAx
2. **Mechanistic Validation**: Using NAMs to validate the mechanistic basis for RAx predictions
3. **Uncertainty Reduction**: Applying NAMs to reduce uncertainty in RAx assessments

## Regulatory Considerations

### ECHA Guidelines

The European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) recognizes the value of NAMs in supporting RAx assessments. The Read-Across Assessment Framework (RAAF) provides guidance on the use of NAMs to enhance the scientific justification for RAx.

### OECD Principles

The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) principles for RAx integration with NAMs include:

1. **Mechanistic Understanding**: Requirement for mechanistic understanding of chemical similarity
2. **Weight of Evidence**: Use of weight of evidence approach to integrate multiple lines of evidence
3. **Transparency**: Transparent documentation of NAM integration and data generation
