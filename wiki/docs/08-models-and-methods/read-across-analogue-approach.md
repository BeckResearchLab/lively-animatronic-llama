## Overview

The analogue approach is a fundamental read-across method that compares a target chemical with one or a few closely related source chemicals to predict properties or toxicological endpoints. This approach is particularly valuable when detailed data is available for a small number of similar chemicals.

## Scope and Notes

This page focuses specifically on the analogue approach within the broader read-across methodology. It covers the definition, methodology, applications, and considerations for using this approach in regulatory and research contexts.

## Key Definitions and Claims

> **Claim**: The analogue approach compares the properties of a substance with a limited number of closely related chemicals (target and source substances).
> **Citation**: [EFSA Read-Across Guidance (2025)](@{LINK}/literature/guidance-on-the-use-of-read-across-for-chemical-safety-assessment-in-food-and-feed.md)
> **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

## Methodology

### Core Principles

1. **Direct Comparison**: Focuses on direct comparison between target and source chemicals
2. **Limited Sources**: Uses one or a few closely related source chemicals
3. **Close Similarity**: Requires high degree of structural or mechanistic similarity
4. **Data Transfer**: Transfers specific data points or properties from source to target

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Target Characterization**: Define the target chemical and its data gaps
2. **Source Identification**: Identify one or a few closely related source chemicals
3. **Similarity Assessment**: Evaluate structural, physicochemical, and mechanistic similarity
4. **Data Evaluation**: Assess quality and relevance of source chemical data
5. **Data Transfer**: Apply source data to fill target chemical data gaps
6. **Uncertainty Assessment**: Evaluate uncertainties in the analogy
7. **Justification**: Document the rationale for the analogue approach

## Applications

### Common Use Cases

- **Specific Endpoint Prediction**: Predicting toxicity for specific endpoints where source data exists
- **Data Gap Filling**: Filling gaps in toxicological data for regulatory submissions
- **Priority Setting**: Supporting chemical prioritization in risk assessment
- **Hazard Classification**: Contributing to hazard classification under regulatory frameworks

### Regulatory Contexts

- **EFSA Food/Food Safety**: Used for novel foods, additives, and contaminants
- **ECHA REACH**: Supports chemical safety assessments under REACH regulation
- **CLP Regulation**: Contributes to classification and labeling decisions
- **Other Jurisdictions**: Adaptable to various regulatory frameworks

## Strengths and Limitations

### Strengths

- **Precision**: High degree of similarity leads to more precise predictions
- **Transparency**: Clear rationale for chemical selection and data transfer
- **Regulatory Acceptance**: Well-established method with regulatory precedence
- **Flexibility**: Can be applied to various endpoints and chemical types

### Limitations

- **Data Dependency**: Requires detailed data on source chemicals
- **Similarity Constraints**: Limited to chemicals with close similarity
- **Endpoint Specificity**: May not generalize across all toxicological endpoints
- **Uncertainty**: Remaining uncertainties need careful assessment and documentation

## Similarity Assessment

Critical factors in analogue approach similarity assessment:

- **Structural Similarity**: Chemical structure comparison and similarity metrics
- **Physicochemical Properties**: Comparison of key physicochemical parameters
- **Mechanistic Similarity**: Evaluation of shared mechanisms of action
- **Toxicokinetic Similarity**: Assessment of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) properties
- **Toxicodynamic Similarity**: Comparison of biological target interactions

## Case Studies and Examples

### Successful Applications

- **Novel Food Additives**: Using analogue approach to predict safety of new food additives
- **Pesticide Residues**: Predicting toxicological properties of pesticide metabolites
- **Industrial Chemicals**: Supporting REACH compliance for data-poor chemicals

### Challenges and Lessons Learned

- **Insufficient Data**: Cases where source chemical data was inadequate
- **Mechanistic Differences**: Unexpected differences in mechanisms despite structural similarity
- **Regulatory Scrutiny**: Cases requiring additional justification for regulatory acceptance
