## Overview

The category approach is a read-across method that groups multiple chemicals based on structural similarity and uses data from the entire category to predict properties of target chemicals. This approach is particularly valuable when dealing with chemical categories or series where multiple source chemicals provide a broader data foundation.

## Scope and Notes

This page focuses specifically on the category approach within the broader read-across methodology. It covers the definition, methodology, applications, and considerations for using this approach in regulatory and research contexts.

## Key Definitions and Claims

> **Claim**: The category approach is based on structural similarity among several source substances to predict the target substance's properties.

## Methodology

### Core Principles

1. **Chemical Categorization**: Groups chemicals based on structural similarity
2. **Multiple Sources**: Uses data from several source chemicals in the category
3. **Pattern Recognition**: Identifies patterns and trends across the category
4. **Statistical Foundation**: Often employs statistical methods for prediction
5. **Hazard Classification**: Particularly suited for hazard classification and categorization

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Category Definition**: Define the chemical category based on structural criteria
2. **Category Membership**: Identify all chemicals belonging to the category
3. **Data Collection**: Gather toxicological data for all category members
4. **Pattern Analysis**: Analyze patterns and trends in the category data
5. **Target Identification**: Identify target chemical(s) within or related to the category
6. **Prediction**: Use category patterns to predict target chemical properties
7. **Uncertainty Assessment**: Evaluate uncertainties in category-based predictions
8. **Justification**: Document the rationale for category formation and predictions

## Applications

### Common Use Cases

- **Hazard Classification**: Supporting classification under CLP and other regulatory frameworks
- **Chemical Categorization**: Grouping chemicals for regulatory assessment
- **Data Gap Filling**: Filling gaps for multiple chemicals in a category
- **Priority Setting**: Supporting chemical prioritization based on category properties
- **Safety Assessment**: Contributing to safety assessments for chemical series

### Regulatory Contexts

- **EFSA Food/Food Safety**: Used for chemical categories in food and feed safety
- **ECHA REACH**: Supports chemical safety assessments under REACH regulation
- **CLP Regulation**: Core method for hazard classification and labeling
- **Other Jurisdictions**: Adaptable to various regulatory frameworks

## Strengths and Limitations

### Strengths

- **Broad Coverage**: Can address multiple chemicals and endpoints simultaneously
- **Pattern Recognition**: Identifies trends and patterns across chemical categories
- **Regulatory Acceptance**: Well-established method for hazard classification
- **Efficiency**: More efficient for assessing multiple chemicals in a category
- **Statistical Foundation**: Provides quantitative basis for predictions

### Limitations

- **Category Definition**: Requires clear and defensible category criteria
- **Data Requirements**: Needs sufficient data across category members
- **Heterogeneity**: Category members may have varying properties
- **Generalization**: Predictions may be less precise than analogue approach
- **Complexity**: More complex methodology and justification required

## Category Formation Criteria

Key factors in defining chemical categories:

- **Structural Similarity**: Chemical structure-based categorization criteria
- **Functional Groups**: Presence of common functional groups or moieties
- **Mechanistic Similarity**: Shared mechanisms of action or biological targets
- **Physicochemical Properties**: Similar physicochemical parameter ranges
- **Toxicological Profile**: Consistent toxicological behavior across category

## Category Types and Examples

### Common Category Types

- **Structural Categories**: Based on core chemical structure and functional groups
- **Mechanistic Categories**: Based on shared mechanisms of action
- **Metabolic Categories**: Based on common metabolic pathways
- **Toxicokinetic Categories**: Based on similar ADME properties
- **Endpoint-Specific Categories**: Focused on specific toxicological endpoints

### Regulatory Examples

- **EFSA Categories**: Chemical categories used in food and feed safety assessments
- **ECHA Categories**: Categories defined under REACH for hazard assessment
- **CLP Categories**: Categories supporting classification and labeling decisions
