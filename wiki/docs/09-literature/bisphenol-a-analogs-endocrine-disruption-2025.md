# Overview

This literature page documents the 2025 review paper titled "Mechanisms of Bisphenol A and Its Analogs as Endocrine Disruptors via Nuclear Receptors and Related Signaling Pathways" by Mark Stanojević and Marija Sollner Dolenc. The paper provides a comprehensive review of the mechanisms by which BPA and its analogs act as endocrine disruptors through various nuclear receptors and signaling pathways.

# Key Claims and Target Pages

This review paper presents several key claims about BPA and its analogs, which have been extracted and targeted to relevant wiki pages:

## Claim 1: BPA Analogs and Endocrine Disruption

**Claim**: BPA analogs can have comparable or even stronger endocrine and toxic effects than BPA.

**Target Page**: `chemicals/bisphenol-a-analogs.md` (to be created)

**Citation**: Stanojević and Sollner Dolenc (2025)

## Claim 2: Mechanisms of Endocrine Disruption

**Claim**: BPA analogs interfere with downstream signaling pathways by binding to nuclear receptors, altering receptor expression, affecting co-receptors, altering signal transduction pathways, and causing epigenetic changes.

**Target Page**: `mechanisms/endocrine-disruption.md` (to be created)

**Citation**: Stanojević and Sollner Dolenc (2025)

## Claim 3: Estrogen Activity

**Claim**: BPA binds weakly to estrogen receptors (ERα and ERβ) with a 10,000-fold lower affinity than 17β-estradiol (E2). Some analogs, like BPAF, show stronger estrogenic activity.

**Target Page**: `receptors/estrogen-receptors.md` (to be created)

**Citation**: Stanojević and Sollner Dolenc (2025); Steinmetz et al. (1997); Kojima et al. (2019)

## Claim 4: Epigenetic Changes

**Claim**: BPA exposure leads to epigenetic changes, such as DNA methylation in the promoter regions of ERα and ERβ, and affects genes like Hoxa10 and GRIK1.

**Target Page**: `mechanisms/epigenetic-changes.md` (to be created)

**Citation**: Stanojević and Sollner Dolenc (2025); Doshi et al. (2011); Bhandari et al. (2019); Khodasevich et al. (2024)

## Claim 5: GPER Signaling

**Claim**: BPA and its analogs activate GPER, leading to rapid signaling events and transcriptional regulation via cAMP and calcium mobilization.

**Target Page**: `receptors/gper.md` (to be created)

**Citation**: Stanojević and Sollner Dolenc (2025); Cheng et al. (2011); Prossnitz and Barton (2011)

## Claim 6: Androgen Receptor Signaling

**Claim**: Some BPA analogs, like BPCcl, exhibit antiandrogenic activity by modulating AR signaling.

**Target Page**: `receptors/androgen-receptors.md` (to be created)

## Claim 7: Thyroid Hormone Receptor Signaling

## Claim 8: PPAR Signaling

# Open Questions

The review highlights several areas that require further research:

- The need for studies investigating the synergistic and additive effects of BPA analogs
- The discrepancy between weak estrogenic activity in vitro and observed effects in vivo
- Long-term epigenetic effects of BPA and its analogs
- Species-specific differences in receptor interactions and signaling pathways
