## Overview

This paper titled "Application of an Accessible Interface for Pharmacokinetic Modeling and In Vitro to In Vivo Extrapolation" provides a detailed case study and technical overview of IVIVE and PBPK modeling, with applications in regulatory decision-making. The paper demonstrates how IVIVE can contextualize in vitro bioactivity data and support regulatory assessments.

## Extracted Key Claims

### Central Claims (Consensus)

1. **Historical Context**: Historically, toxicity testing has relied on laboratory animal-based methods, but ethical and practical concerns have driven interest in non-animal approaches like in vitro assays and computational models.

2. **Regulatory Drivers**: The Toxic Substances Control Act amendment calls for the development of new approach methodologies (NAMs) to reduce dependence on animal testing.

3. **IVIVE Role**: In vitro assays can rapidly evaluate bioactivity across broad chemical sets, but their results need to be translated to organism-level exposures using in vitro to in vivo extrapolation (IVIVE).

4. **PBPK Requirements**: PBPK models require chemical-specific ADME and physicochemical parameters, such as intrinsic clearance, plasma protein fraction unbound, and tissue-specific partition coefficients.

5. **QSAR Integration**: QSAR modeling can predict PBPK parameters where experimental data is limited, broadening the applicability of PBPK approaches.

6. **Regulatory Applications**: IVIVE can support regulatory decision-making by informing experimental design, incorporating ADME processes, and enabling margin-of-exposure assessments.

7. **Data Interpretation**: IVIVE facilitates the interpretation of in vitro data by providing in vivo context, which is essential for decision-making.

8. **Case Study**: A case study comparing dodecyltrimethylammonium chloride (DTAC) and 2-chloro-n-phenylacetamide (CNPA) demonstrated how IVIVE can contextualize in vitro bioactivity.

9. **Regulatory Use**: PBPK modeling and IVIVE are increasingly used in regulatory contexts to support hazard and risk screening, exposure-based assessments, and data gap filling.

10. **Model Types**: Generalized PBPK models facilitate broad chemical screening, while chemical-specific models provide more precise results for regulatory decision-making.

11. **Tools**: Tools like IVIVE and the Integrated Chemical Environment (ICE) make mechanistic in vitro data more accessible and interpretable for regulatory use.

### Supporting Claims (Consensus)

12. **DTAC vs CNPA**: DTAC and CNPA had similar in vitro bioactivity but differed in their estimated administered doses (EADs) due to differences in ADME properties.
