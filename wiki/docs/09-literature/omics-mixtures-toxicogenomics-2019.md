# The State-of-the-Art of Environmental Toxicogenomics: Challenges and Perspectives of 'Omics' Approaches Directed to Toxicant Mixtures

## Source Metadata

- **Authors**: Carla Martins, Kristian Dreij, Pedro M. Costa
- **Year**: 2019
- **Source Type**: Review paper
- **Ingestion Strategy**: B (Argument-centric Extraction)
- **DOI**: Not provided in the chunks

## Summary

This review paper examines the application of 'omics' approaches (genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics) to the study of toxicant mixtures in environmental toxicology. It discusses the challenges and perspectives of using these high-throughput technologies to understand the complex effects of chemical mixtures on biological systems.

## Key Themes

### Toxicology of Mixtures

- The toxicology of mixtures presents significant challenges, particularly for environmental toxicologists dealing with unknown chemicals
- 'Omics' methods enable integration of toxicokinetics and toxicodynamics with mechanistic insights

### 'Omics' Technologies

- **Genomics and epigenomics**: Lagging in toxicology due to high costs and limited genomic annotation
- **Transcriptomics**: Widely used for global gene expression analysis using microarrays and RNA-Seq
- **Proteomics**: Provides overview of metabolic conditions and is used in systems toxicology
- **Metabolomics**: Complements other omics approaches by providing functional readouts

### Systems Toxicology

- Aims to build predictive models from integrated biological data
- Combines multiple omics datasets to understand complex toxicological responses

### Challenges

- Technical demands and high costs of 'omics' approaches
- Need for validation with single-endpoint methods
- Data complexity and integration challenges
- Computational and bioinformatics requirements

### Applications to Mixtures

- 'Omics' methods applied to study binary and complex mixtures
- Identification of biomarkers and pathways affected by mixtures
- Integration with adverse outcome pathways (AOPs)
