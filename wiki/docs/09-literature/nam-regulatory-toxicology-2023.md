## Overview

This review paper titled "New approach methodologies in human regulatory toxicology – Not if, but how and when!" provides a comprehensive overview of the challenges and opportunities for integrating new approach methodologies (NAMs) into regulatory toxicology, with a focus on transitioning towards next-generation risk assessment (NGRA).

## Scope and Source Notes

This review paper focuses on:
- The current paradigm of chemical risk assessment and its limitations
- Challenges for implementing NAMs in regulatory toxicology
- Possible ways forward for standardization, validation, and harmonization
- The role of NAMs in mixture toxicity assessment
- The transition towards next-generation risk assessment (NGRA)
- Regulatory changes needed to support NAM adoption

## Extracted Key Claims

### Central Claims (Consensus)

1. **NAMs Potential**: NAMs have the potential to fundamentally transform regulatory work by allowing more human-relevant decision-making in hazard and exposure assessment, but several obstacles hamper their broader application.

2. **Current Paradigm Limitations**: The current animal-centric paradigm faces challenges in addressing repeated-dose toxicity, chronic toxicity, and emerging endpoints like developmental neurotoxicity and immunotoxicity.

3. **AOP Integration**: NAMs can be combined with in vivo test methods and clinical observations to build and expand adverse outcome pathways (AOPs), providing mechanistic insights and enhancing predictive capacity.

4. **Regulatory Challenges**: Data produced by NAMs alone are currently not perceived as sufficient for broad spectrum chemical safety endpoints, and regulatory frameworks need adaptation to accommodate NAM-based approaches.

5. **Validation Needs**: For wider acceptance of NAMs, it is imperative to demonstrate robustness, reproducibility, biological relevance, and fitness for intended purpose.

### Supporting Claims (Consensus)

6. **Skin Sensitization**: NAMs for skin sensitization have been thoroughly addressed by OECD, with three mechanistically-based test methods incorporated into defined approaches.

7. **Mixture Toxicity**: Mixture effects are most probable when substances share the same molecular target, and NAMs can help identify priority mixtures for regulatory scrutiny.

8. **Omics Technologies**: Omics technologies enable insights into complex biological responses and can be used for read-across and biomarker development.

9. **Standardization**: Multiple initiatives have published guidance documents and reporting templates to promote reproducibility and robustness of NAMs.

10. **Regulatory Flexibility**: The US regulatory system is more flexible for NAM adoption compared to Europe, where rigid information requirements hinder NAM implementation.

### Open Questions (Contested)

11. **General Skepticism**: A general skepticism towards NAMs persists due to limitations in organismal complexity, missing toxicokinetic context, and uncertainty about predictive reliability.

12. **Validation Paradigm**: The traditional validation process needs to undergo paradigm change to allow for more timely acceptance of NAMs.

13. **Regulatory Transition**: The transition to NGRA requires not only scientific development but also strategic roadmaps for legislative changes.
