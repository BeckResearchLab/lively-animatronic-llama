# Overview

This literature page summarizes the key findings from the review paper "Good Read-across Practices" by Thomas Hartung and Costanza Rovida (2025). The paper provides a comparative analysis of three major read-across frameworks: EFSA's 2025 guidance, ECHA's RAAF, and the GRAP principles.

## Source Metadata

- **Title**: Good Read-across Practices
- **Authors**: Thomas Hartung, Costanza Rovida
- **Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: 10.3389/ftox.2025.1690491
- **Container**: Frontiers in Toxicology
- **Source Type**: Review paper (technical)
- **Ingestion Strategy**: C (Mechanism or Case Extraction)

## Key Themes and Claims

### Read-across Frameworks

#### EFSA 2025 Guidance

> **Claim**: EFSA's 2025 guidance provides a seven-step workflow for food and feed safety, emphasizing transparency, NAM integration, and uncertainty analysis.
> **Citation**: [cit-001](#citation-001)

The EFSA guidance introduces a structured approach to read-across that includes:
- Clear problem formulation
- Systematic source substance identification
- Mechanistic similarity assessment
- Integration of new approach methods (NAMs)
- Comprehensive uncertainty analysis
- Transparent documentation

#### ECHA RAAF

> **Claim**: ECHA's RAAF is a scenario-based evaluator tool for REACH, focusing on scientific justification and consistency in regulatory scrutiny.
> **Citation**: [cit-002](#citation-002)

The RAAF framework provides:
- Scenario-based assessment elements
- Scientific justification requirements
- Consistency in regulatory evaluation
- Integration with REACH compliance requirements
- Structured reporting templates

#### GRAP Principles

> **Claim**: GRAP establishes best-practice principles for read-across, emphasizing mechanistic similarity, weight-of-evidence, and transparency.
> **Citation**: [cit-003](#citation-003)

The GRAP principles include:
- Mechanistic plausibility requirements
- Weight-of-evidence approaches
- Transparency in methodology
- Standardized reporting formats
- Best-practice guidelines for regulatory submissions

### Scientific Rigor

#### Biological Similarity

> **Claim**: All frameworks emphasize the need for mechanistic plausibility, with GRAP advocating for bioactivity-based read-across.
> **Citation**: [cit-004](#citation-004)

Key aspects of biological similarity assessment:
- Structural similarity requirements
- Mechanistic plausibility evaluation
- Bioactivity-based approaches (GRAP)
- Cross-species extrapolation considerations

#### ADME Considerations

> **Claim**: Differences in toxicokinetics must be addressed to ensure valid read-across predictions.
> **Citation**: [cit-005](#citation-005)

ADME considerations include:
- Absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion differences
- Species-specific toxicokinetic variations
- Route-of-exposure adjustments
- Metabolite profiling requirements

#### Uncertainty and Weight-of-Evidence

> **Claim**: EFSA and GRAP explicitly address uncertainty, while RAAF integrates it into assessment elements.
> **Citation**: [cit-006](#citation-006)

Uncertainty handling approaches:
- EFSA: Dedicated uncertainty analysis section
- GRAP: Weight-of-evidence framework
- RAAF: Integrated uncertainty assessment elements
- Quantitative uncertainty characterization methods

### NAMs and AOPs

> **Claim**: EFSA and GRAP strongly encourage the use of NAMs and AOPs to strengthen read-across arguments.
> **Citation**: [cit-007](#citation-007)

NAM and AOP integration:
- In vitro assay data incorporation
- Computational model validation
- Adverse outcome pathway mapping
- Mechanistic data integration
- Alternative test method acceptance

### Practical Implementation

#### Case Studies

> **Claim**: EFSA and GRAP provide examples and case studies to illustrate successful read-across applications.
> **Citation**: [cit-008](#citation-008)

Case study examples include:
- Food additive safety assessments
- Pesticide residue evaluations
- Industrial chemical categorization
- Regulatory submission scenarios

#### Tools and Templates

> **Claim**: EFSA offers structured templates and tools, while GRAP proposes standardized reporting formats.
> **Citation**: [cit-009](#citation-009)

Implementation tools:
- EFSA: Data matrices, uncertainty assessment templates
- GRAP: Reporting format guidelines
- RAAF: Scenario evaluator tool
- Cross-framework compatibility considerations

### Regulatory Acceptance

> **Claim**: EFSA's guidance signals a shift toward formal acceptance of read-across in food/feed risk assessments.
> **Citation**: [cit-010](#citation-010)

Regulatory impact:
- EFSA: Formal acceptance in food/feed safety
- RAAF: Improved quality and acceptance under REACH
- GRAP: Influence on best-practice standards
- International harmonization potential
