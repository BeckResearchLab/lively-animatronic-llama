## Abstract

This review article explores the integration of Physiologically Based Pharmacokinetic (PBPK) models with New Approach Methodologies (NAMs) for risk assessment of pharmaceuticals and environmental chemicals. The article discusses how PBPK models enhance adverse outcome pathway (AOP) development, particularly quantitative AOPs (qAOPs), and their applications in predicting toxicity endpoints such as developmental neurotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and cardiotoxicity. The review also covers the role of machine learning in improving PBPK modeling and the use of in vitro systems for predicting molecular initiating events and key events.

## Key Topics Covered

1. Integration of PBPK models with adverse outcome pathways (AOPs)
2. Development of quantitative adverse outcome pathways (qAOPs)
3. Applications of PBPK models in risk assessment
4. Role of machine learning in PBPK modeling
5. Use of in vitro systems for predicting molecular initiating events
6. Multi-compartment PBPK models for tissue-specific analysis
7. Organ-specific PBPK models for AOP development
8. Applications in sensitive populations (infants, diseased patients)
