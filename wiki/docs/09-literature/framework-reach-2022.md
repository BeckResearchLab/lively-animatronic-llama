## Summary

This paper presents a comprehensive framework for chemical safety assessment that incorporates new approach methodologies (NAMs) within the REACH regulation. The authors describe an emerging consensus on how to use information from in silico, in vitro, and targeted in vivo sources to assess chemical safety, addressing the limitations of traditional observational trial approaches.

## Key Themes

### Emerging Consensus on NAMs

The paper highlights the growing recognition of NAMs as valid approaches for chemical safety assessment, supported by long-term investment in these methodologies across the EU and worldwide.

### Framework Structure

The proposed framework incorporates:
- In silico methods for initial screening and hazard identification
- In vitro methods for mechanistic confirmation and targeted assessment
- Targeted in vivo methods for final validation and risk assessment

### Tiered Approach

The framework uses a tiered approach where:
- Each tier produces specific outputs (classification categories, safe doses, risk assessments)
- Progression through tiers depends on outputs from previous tiers
- The process can stop when an acceptable profile is reached or when it's determined that no acceptable profile would result

### Transparency and Decision-Making

The framework emphasizes:
- Transparent and phased introduction of NAMs
- Science-based safety decisions
- Clear documentation of assessment processes

## Extracted Claims

### Core Claims

1. **Emerging Consensus**: The long-term investment in NAMs is resulting in an emerging consensus on how to use information from in silico, in vitro, and targeted in vivo sources to assess chemical safety.

2. **Framework Structure**: A framework incorporating in silico, in vitro, and in vivo methods designed to meet REACH requirements, assessing both hazard and exposure using a tiered approach.

3. **Tiered Outputs**: The outputs from each tier are classification categories, safe doses, and risk assessments, with progression through tiers depending on previous tier outputs.

4. **Transparency**: The framework allows transparent and phased introduction of NAMs, enabling science-based safety decisions.

5. **Observational Trials Limitations**: Current methodology is based on observational trials using doses much greater than applicable to mimic human exposure.

6. **Knowledge Accumulation**: A vast body of knowledge has accumulated from observational trials, realizing that adverse outcomes are caused by effects on biological processes.

7. **Tiered Prediction**: Tiered approaches are based on the assumption that adverse outcomes can be predicted with greater precision as the process proceeds from in silico to in vitro to in vivo.

8. **Stopping Criteria**: The process could stop when an acceptable profile was reached, or when it was determined that no acceptable profile would result.

9. **Current REACH Requirements**: Current approach mandates lists of studies addressing potential hazards, with extent decided by annual production tonnage.

10. **Exposure-Based Assessment**: Need for agreed starting process based on broad exposure description and hazard information requirements based on known exposure.
