## Overview

This guidance document from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) provides comprehensive framework for applying read-across methods in food and feed safety assessments. It represents a significant step toward formalizing read-across as an accepted approach in regulatory toxicology for food safety applications.

## Scope and Source Notes

- **Target Audience**: Risk assessors, applicants, and regulatory bodies involved in chemical safety assessment for food and feed
- **Scope**: Applicable to individual substances, not chemical mixtures. Focuses on food and feed safety but is broadly applicable to other chemical risk assessments
- **Key Topics**: Read-across approach, problem formulation, data gap analysis, source substance identification, uncertainty assessment, and documentation

## Extracted Key Claims

1. **Definition**: Read-across is an approach used in chemical risk assessment for screening, classification, prioritization, and hazard assessment of substances based on toxicological data of similar chemicals.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

2. **Alternative to Testing**: Read-across is one of the most common alternatives to animal testing, providing opportunities for predicting toxicological responses for data-poor chemicals.
   - **Verification Status**: ⚠️ Overstated - The claim is supported but the "most common" qualifier is not explicitly stated in the source
   - **Recommendation**: Remove or qualify the "most common" assertion

3. **Methods**: The read-across approach includes two main methods: the analogue approach and the category approach.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

4. **Analogue Approach**: The analogue approach compares the properties of a substance with a limited number of closely related chemicals (target and source substances).
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

5. **Category Approach**: The category approach is based on structural similarity among several source substances to predict the target substance's properties.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

6. **Workflow**: The read-across workflow includes steps such as problem formulation, data gap analysis, source substance identification and evaluation, data gap filling, uncertainty assessment, and documentation.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

7. **Regulatory Applications**: Read-across can be used for hazard-based classification and labeling within the CLP Regulation as part of a weight-of-evidence assessment.
   - **Verification Status**: ⚠️ Partially supported - The claim about CLP Regulation is not explicitly stated in the source
   - **Recommendation**: Remove or qualify the CLP Regulation reference

8. **Regulatory Acceptability**: The acceptability of read-across predictions may vary according to the regulatory context, depending on the problem formulation.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported

9. **EFSA Remit**: EFSA's remit includes risk assessment and regulatory advice on the safety of a broad range of substances and foodstuffs, such as novel foods, food and feed additives, and pesticides.
   - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported
   - **Note**: The specific list of substances comes from EFSA's general remit rather than this specific guidance document

10. **Data Gap Filling**: Read-across is seen as a possible way to fill gaps in toxicological data for substances where testing is not feasible or economically viable.
    - **Verification Status**: ✅ Supported
