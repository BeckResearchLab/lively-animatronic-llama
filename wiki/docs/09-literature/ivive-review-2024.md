## Extracted Key Claims

### Central Claims (Consensus)

1. **IVIVE in NGRA**: IVIVE is a key step in next-generation risk assessment that translates bioactive chemical concentrations from in vitro assays to in vivo exposures using physiologically-based toxicokinetic (PBTK) models and machine learning (ML) algorithms.

2. **NGRA Overview**: NGRA integrates new approach methodologies (NAMs) such as in silico and in vitro approaches to make health and safety decisions without relying on in vivo data.

3. **PBTK Models**: PBTK models provide a quantitative description of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) processes and are used to correlate environmental exposure concentrations with target chemical concentrations in organisms.

4. **ML in IVIVE**: ML algorithms are used in IVIVE to predict in vivo toxicity by combining chemical structure characterization with in vitro high-throughput screening (HTS) assay data. However, ML models have limitations such as limited interpretability.

5. **AOP Framework**: The AOP framework provides a theoretical basis for IVIVE and enhances the predictive capabilities of ML algorithms by organizing toxicology knowledge mechanistically.

6. **Future Directions**: Future research should expand the scope of IVIVE to include metabolite toxicity, susceptible populations, and the integration of new technologies such as omics data.

### Supporting Claims (Consensus)

7. **HTS Initiatives**: HTS initiatives, such as ToxCast and Tox21, have generated extensive in vitro toxicity data, promoting the development of IVIVE.

8. **Regulatory Initiatives**: Regulatory agencies, such as the U.S. EPA and EU authorities, have promoted the development and validation of alternative methods for animal testing, including IVIVE.

9. **Toxicity Endpoints**: IVIVE studies have focused on predicting various toxicity endpoints, including neurotoxicity, developmental toxicity, hepatotoxicity, and endocrine effects.

### Open Questions (Contested)

10. **IVIVE Limitations**: Current PBTK model-based IVIVE studies primarily focus on parent compounds, with limited studies on metabolites. Additionally, the interpretability of ML models remains a challenge.
