# Overview

Bisphenol AF (BPAF) is a fluorinated derivative of Bisphenol A (BPA) used in polycarbonate copolymers and high-temperature composites. As a BPA analog, BPAF exhibits endocrine-disrupting properties and interacts with estrogen receptors, often showing stronger agonistic activity than BPA itself.

# Evidence and Details

## Chemical Properties

- **Chemical formula**: C₁₅H₁₄F₂O₂
- **Molecular weight**: 262.27 g/mol
- **CAS Number**: 1478-61-1
- **Structure**: Fluorinated derivative of BPA with fluorine atoms replacing hydrogen atoms on the central carbon

## Industrial Applications

BPAF is used in:
- Polycarbonate copolymers
- High-temperature composites
- As a potential substitute for BPA in certain applications due to its different chemical properties

## Endocrine Disruption Profile

BPAF exhibits a distinct endocrine disruption profile compared to BPA:

- **ERα activity**: Stronger agonistic activity than BPA
- **ERβ activity**: Shows antagonist activity in certain cell lines
- **Mechanistic differences**: Activates different functional domains of estrogen receptors
- **Gene expression**: Induces expression of key ER target genes

## Toxicological Effects

While the specific toxicological profile of BPAF is still being investigated, its stronger estrogenic activity suggests potential for:
- Reproductive system effects
- Developmental effects
- Metabolic effects
- Potential carcinogenic effects
