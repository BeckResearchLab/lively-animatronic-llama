## Overview

Quantitative In Vitro to In Vivo Extrapolation (QIVIVE) is a method used to predict the in vivo effects of chemicals based on in vitro data. This approach bridges the gap between in vitro assays and in vivo outcomes, supporting the development of alternative methods to animal testing.

## Scope and Notes

This page defines the principles and applications of QIVIVE. It does not cover the implementation or validation of QIVIVE methods, which are addressed in separate pages.

## Key Claims or Definitions

### Definition of QIVIVE

QIVIVE is a method that:

1. Uses in vitro data to predict in vivo effects.
2. Incorporates physiological and pharmacokinetic models to extrapolate from in vitro to in vivo.
3. Supports the development of alternative methods to animal testing.

### Key Components of QIVIVE

1. **In Vitro Data**: Data obtained from in vitro assays (e.g., toxicity, activity).
2. **Physiological Models**: Models of physiological processes (e.g., absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion).
3. **Pharmacokinetic Models**: Models of the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of chemicals.
4. **Extrapolation**: The process of predicting in vivo effects based on in vitro data and physiological/pharmacokinetic models.
5. **Validation**: Assessment of the QIVIVE model's performance and reliability.

## Evidence or Details

### Principles of QIVIVE

1. **In Vitro to In Vivo Extrapolation**: Using in vitro data to predict in vivo effects by accounting for physiological and pharmacokinetic processes.
2. **Physiological Modeling**: Incorporating models of physiological processes to improve the accuracy of extrapolations.
3. **Pharmacokinetic Modeling**: Using pharmacokinetic models to predict the absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion of chemicals.
4. **Validation**: Assessing the QIVIVE model's performance using appropriate metrics and experimental data.

### Applications of QIVIVE

- **Toxicity Prediction**: Predicting the in vivo toxicity of chemicals based on in vitro data.
- **Alternative Methods**: Supporting the development of alternative methods to animal testing.
- **Regulatory Assessment**: Providing data to support regulatory decisions.
- **Risk Assessment**: Evaluating the potential risks of chemicals to human health and the environment.
