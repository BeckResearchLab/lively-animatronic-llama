## Overview

Weight of evidence (WoE) refers to the overall body of evidence supporting or refuting a particular conclusion or hypothesis. It is a critical concept in scientific assessment, particularly in toxicology and risk assessment, where multiple sources of data must be synthesized to inform decisions.

## Scope and Notes

This page defines weight of evidence, explains its role in scientific assessments, and provides context for its use in evaluating the strength of evidence.

## Evidence or Details

### Components of Weight of Evidence

Weight of evidence is determined by several key components:

1. **Relevance**: The pertinence of the evidence to the question or hypothesis being addressed.
2. **Quality**: The rigor, reliability, and validity of the evidence.
3. **Consistency**: The degree to which different sources of evidence agree or disagree.
4. **Quantity**: The amount of evidence available.
5. **Temporal and Geographical Coverage**: The range of conditions under which the evidence was gathered.

### Methods for Assessing Weight of Evidence

- **Narrative Reviews**: Qualitative synthesis of evidence.
- **Meta-Analysis**: Statistical synthesis of quantitative data.
- **Structured Weight of Evidence Frameworks**: Systematic approaches to evaluating evidence, such as those used by regulatory agencies.

### Factors Influencing Weight of Evidence

- **Study Design**: Randomized controlled trials are generally considered more robust than observational studies.
- **Sample Size**: Larger sample sizes provide more reliable estimates.
- **Publication Bias**: The tendency for positive results to be published more frequently than negative results.
- **Confounding Variables**: Factors that may influence the relationship between exposure and effect.
