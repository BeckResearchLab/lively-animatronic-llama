# Evidence Standards

This page defines the standards for evidence quality and acceptance within the wiki. These standards ensure that all claims are supported by reliable, verifiable, and relevant sources.

## Overview

Evidence standards apply to all substantive claims made in the wiki. Claims must be supported by sources that meet the criteria outlined in this document. Non-compliance with these standards may result in claims being marked as unsupported or requiring revision.

## Evidence Quality Criteria

### Source Types

The following source types are accepted for evidence:

- **Primary Research Papers**: Peer-reviewed articles reporting original research.
- **Reviews and Meta-Analyses**: Comprehensive reviews or meta-analyses of primary research.
- **Reports**: Reports from regulatory agencies, government bodies, or reputable organizations.
- **Datasets**: Structured data from reliable sources, such as public databases or research repositories.
- **Books and Book Chapters**: Authoritative texts in the field of toxicology or related disciplines.
- **Evidence Pages**: Structured evidence pages within the wiki that synthesize claims from multiple sources.

### Source Reliability

Sources must be reliable and credible. Reliability is determined by the following factors:

- **Peer Review**: Sources must be peer-reviewed unless they are reports from reputable organizations or datasets from trusted repositories.
- **Authoritative Authors**: Sources should be authored by recognized experts in the field.
- **Reputation of Publisher**: Sources should be published in journals, by organizations, or in repositories with a strong reputation for quality.
- **Accessibility**: Sources must be accessible under the wiki's source policy. Restricted sources may be cited but require additional justification.

### Relevance and Scope

Evidence must be relevant to the claim and appropriately scoped. Irrelevant or overly broad sources are not acceptable. For example:

- A claim about a chemical's toxicity in humans must be supported by evidence from human studies or studies in relevant animal models.
- A claim about a specific endpoint must be supported by evidence directly related to that endpoint.

### Temporal Relevance

Evidence should be recent enough to be considered current. The acceptable timeframe depends on the field and the nature of the claim. Generally:

- For rapidly evolving fields, evidence should be within the last 5 years.
- For more stable fields, evidence within the last 10 years may be acceptable.

## Claim Verification Process

### Verification Steps

1. **Identify Claims**: Locate all substantive claims on the page.
2. **Check Citations**: Ensure each claim is supported by at least one citation.
3. **Evaluate Sources**: Verify that the cited sources meet the evidence quality criteria.
4. **Assess Relevance**: Confirm that the evidence is relevant and appropriately scoped.
5. **Determine Verification Status**: Assign a verification status to each claim based on the evidence.

### Verification Statuses

- **Supported**: The claim is fully supported by the cited evidence.
- **Unsupported**: The claim lacks sufficient evidence or the evidence is insufficient.
- **Overstated**: The evidence supports a narrower or more qualified version of the claim.
- **Contradicted**: The evidence contradicts the claim.
- **Source Inaccessible**: The supporting evidence could not be accessed under the wiki's source policy.
- **Needs Human Review**: The claim requires expert interpretation or adjudication.

## Handling Contradictions

If evidence contradicts a claim, the following steps should be taken:

1. **Identify the Contradiction**: Clearly document the contradiction and the conflicting evidence.
2. **Review the Claim**: Determine whether the claim should be revised, removed, or retained with additional context.
3. **Update the Claim**: If the claim is retained, add qualifiers or notes to address the contradiction.
4. **Audit the Change**: Record the contradiction and resolution in the audit trail.

## Compliance and Enforcement

All pages must comply with these evidence standards. Non-compliance may result in:

- Claims being marked as unsupported or requiring revision.
- Pages being marked as needing review or human review.
- Restrictions on the use of claims in synthesis or decision-making.
