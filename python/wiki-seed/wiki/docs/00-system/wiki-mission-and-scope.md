# Mission

The Computational Toxicology Wiki is a curated, agent-readable knowledge base designed to support the interpretation, synthesis, and verification of toxicological information. It serves as a central repository for structured data, evidence claims, and operational workflows, enabling both human and automated agents to efficiently navigate and contribute to the field of computational toxicology.

## Core Objectives

1. **Knowledge Integration**: Consolidate dispersed toxicological knowledge into a structured, interlinked format.
2. **Evidence Traceability**: Ensure all substantive claims are grounded in verifiable sources and evidence.
3. **Agent Readability**: Provide a format and structure optimized for automated retrieval, routing, and synthesis.
4. **Collaborative Maintenance**: Facilitate contributions from both human experts and automated agents while ensuring quality and consistency.

# Scope

## In Scope

- **Toxicological Concepts**: Definitions and explanations of key terms, methods, and frameworks used in computational toxicology.
- **Chemicals and Substances**: Canonical pages for chemicals, including identifiers, relevant endpoints, and evidence links.
- **Toxicological Endpoints**: Pages describing adverse outcomes, hazard classes, and evidence summaries.
- **Assays and Methods**: Descriptions of experimental and in silico assays, including their interpretation and limitations.
- **Datasets and Resources**: Documentation of data resources, schemas, provenance, and access routes.
- **Models and Methods**: Descriptions of computational models and analytical methods used in toxicology.
- **Workflows**: Procedural pages for repeatable human or agent workflows, including ingestion, analysis, and assessment.
- **Governance and Quality**: Policies and standards for evidence quality, citation rules, and responsible use.
- **Indices and Navigation**: Curated navigation pages to facilitate discovery and retrieval.

## Out of Scope

- **Raw Data Storage**: The wiki does not store raw experimental or computational data; it links to datasets and resources.
- **Primary Literature**: While literature summaries are included, the wiki does not replicate full-text articles.
- **Regulatory Decisions**: The wiki provides evidence and context but does not make or endorse regulatory decisions.
- **General Biological Knowledge**: Only toxicology-specific biological context is included; general biology is assumed to be known.

# Target Audience

- **Research Agents**: Automated systems that retrieve, synthesize, and verify toxicological information.
- **Human Researchers**: Scientists and experts seeking structured, interlinked toxicological knowledge.
- **Policy Makers**: Individuals requiring evidence-based summaries for decision-making.
- **Developers**: Those building tools or workflows that interact with toxicological data.

# Governance and Maintenance

The wiki is maintained under a set of governance rules designed to ensure quality, consistency, and reliability. These rules include:

- **Evidence Standards**: Criteria for acceptable evidence and citation practices.
- **Verification Protocols**: Procedures for verifying claims and resolving contradictions.
- **Review Checkpoints**: Points at which human review is required for critical content.
- **Deprecation Policy**: Guidelines for retiring or updating outdated content.

For detailed governance policies, see the [Quality and Governance](14-quality-and-governance) section.

# Future Directions

The wiki is designed to evolve with the field of computational toxicology. Future expansions may include:

- **Expanded Chemical Coverage**: Inclusion of additional chemicals and mixtures.
- **Advanced Workflows**: Integration of more complex agentic workflows for synthesis and prediction.
- **Interoperability**: Enhanced compatibility with external knowledge graphs and ontologies.
- **User Contributions**: Mechanisms for community-driven content contributions and reviews.
