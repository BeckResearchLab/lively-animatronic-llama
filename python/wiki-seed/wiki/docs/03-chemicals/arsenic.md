# Arsenic

## Overview
Arsenic (As) is a naturally occurring metalloid found in the Earth's crust. It is a well-known toxicant with significant public health implications due to its widespread presence in the environment and its harmful effects on human health. Arsenic exposure primarily occurs through contaminated water, food, and air, and it is classified as a human carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)cit-001.

## Evidence and Details

### Toxicity and Health Effects
Arsenic is a potent toxicant with well-documented adverse effects on human health. Chronic exposure to arsenic has been linked to various types of cancer, including skin, lung, and bladder cancer. Additionally, arsenic exposure during developmental stages can lead to cognitive impairments and developmental delays in children. The neurotoxic effects of arsenic are also significant, with studies showing its impact on the nervous systemcit-001,cit-002.

### Mechanisms of Toxicity
The toxic mechanisms of arsenic involve the generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), leading to oxidative stress. This oxidative stress damages cellular components, including lipids, proteins, and DNA. Arsenic also interferes with cellular metabolism by inhibiting key enzymes and disrupting mitochondrial function. These mechanisms collectively contribute to the carcinogenic and cytotoxic effects of arseniccit-003,cit-004.

### Environmental Exposure
Arsenic contamination of water and soil is a global concern, particularly in regions with naturally high arsenic levels or industrial pollution. Contaminated water used for drinking and irrigation is a primary route of exposure, leading to widespread health issues in affected populations. Soil contamination can also lead to the accumulation of arsenic in food crops, further exacerbating exposure riskscit-005,cit-006.
