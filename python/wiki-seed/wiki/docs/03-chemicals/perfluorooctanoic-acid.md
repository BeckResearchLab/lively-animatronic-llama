# Perfluorooctanoic Acid

## Overview
Perfluorooctanoic Acid (PFOA), also known as C8, is a synthetic chemical belonging to the class of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS). It is widely used in industrial applications and consumer products due to its surfactant properties and resistance to heat and oil. PFOA has gained significant attention due to its persistence in the environment and potential toxicological effects on human health.

## Evidence and Details

### Toxicological Effects
PFOA has been shown to induce hepatointestinal toxicity in broilers, as evidenced by decreased antioxidant enzyme activity and increased pro-inflammatory cytokine levels. This toxicity is mediated through the inhibition of the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, which plays a crucial role in cellular survival and proliferation. Additionally, PFOA exposure has been linked to immune system disruption, including decreased humoral and adaptive immune responses, as well as reduced antibody response to vaccinations such as COVID-19 in children. These effects highlight the broad toxicological impact of PFOA on various biological systems.

### Carcinogenic Potential
PFOA is classified as a potential carcinogen, with evidence linking it to bladder carcinogenesis. Studies have identified molecular signatures and pathways that converge between PFOA-associated toxicological effects and bladder cancer biology. For instance, a nine-gene classifier derived from PFOA-associated targets has shown excellent discriminatory performance in identifying bladder cancer, suggesting a strong association between PFOA exposure and carcinogenic processes.

### Mechanistic Pathways
The toxicological effects of PFOA are mediated through various mechanistic pathways. For example, PFOA-induced toxicity is linked to the inhibition of the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway, which is critical for cellular survival and proliferation. Additionally, PFOA exposure disrupts lipid metabolism, potentially contributing to carcinogenesis by impairing dietary fatty acid uptake and de novo lipid synthesis, leading to abnormal lipid accumulation and oxidative stress.

### Immune System Disruption
PFOA exposure has been associated with immune system disruption, including decreased humoral and adaptive immune responses. In silico analyses have identified key hub genes such as IFNG, TNF, and IL1B as common targets of PFOA, indicating its role in immune modulation and cytokine signaling. These findings suggest that PFOA may contribute to the development of autoimmune disorders and other immune-related diseases.

### Impact on Vaccine Response
PFOA exposure has been shown to reduce antibody response to COVID-19 vaccination in children. This effect is likely mediated through the disruption of immune system function, as evidenced by decreased humoral immune responses. These findings underscore the importance of considering environmental exposures such as PFOA when evaluating vaccine efficacy.

### Lipid Metabolism Disruption
PFOA exposure disrupts lipid metabolism, potentially contributing to carcinogenesis. This disruption is mediated through the activation of lipid-sensitive nuclear receptors and the dysregulation of key transcription factors such as peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) and sterol regulatory element-binding proteins (SREBPs). These alterations can lead to abnormal lipid accumulation, oxidative stress, and the activation of pro-oncogenic signaling pathways.

### Prostate Cancer Association
PFOA exposure is associated with an increased risk of prostate cancer. Multi-omics and computational toxicology approaches have identified shared molecular targets between PFOA and prostate cancer, including genes involved in inflammation, oxidative stress, and cancer-related pathways such as PPAR and p53 signaling. These findings suggest that PFOA may contribute to prostate cancer progression through the dysregulation of core genes and critical cancer pathways.
